## Opening Hook

Listening is the GCSE Music skill nobody quite knows how to teach. It happens in real time — a student hears something once, struggles to name it, and the moment is gone before they've understood it. EchoAural slows that moment down, without taking the music out of it.

Students don't memorise definitions. They listen to real musical extracts — real recordings, not synthesised MIDI — and learn to recognise what they're hearing: melody, intervals, instruments, texture, rhythm, harmony, structure, expression. Then they do it again, and again, until recognition stops feeling like guessing and starts feeling like knowing.

**Listen. Identify. Improve.** That's the whole method — and it's built to hold up under exam pressure, not just in a quiet classroom.

## Why It Works (The EchoAural Method)

**Progress that has to be earned, not guessed.** A student doesn't "level up" in EchoAural because they got a lucky streak. Every level requires a solid volume of practice, a genuinely high accuracy rate held across a *variety* of different questions — and even then, if one specific sub-skill is quietly lagging behind while the rest look fine, the system holds that student back until it catches up. No skill gets to hide behind a good average.

**Mistakes come back — on purpose.** Get something wrong, and EchoAural doesn't just move on. It quietly brings that exact gap back a little later — not immediately (so it isn't just short-term memory), and not too far off either — at just the spacing that makes it actually stick.

**One skill, tracked everywhere it shows up.** Most platforms track "how did you do in this app." EchoAural tracks the underlying musical skill itself, wherever it appears — so a teacher's view of a student's cadence recognition, say, is built from every activity that ever touched it, not siloed app by app.

**A human is always the one who decides.** EchoAural surfaces what a student needs next — it never assigns it automatically. Every recommendation is there for a teacher to review and act on, or not. The system supports the teacher's judgement; it doesn't replace it.

**Real music, not simulations.** Every extract is a real recording, rights-cleared and reviewed by a person before it's used — because the exam won't play a synthesiser, and neither do we.
